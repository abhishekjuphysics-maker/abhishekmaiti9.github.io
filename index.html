Academic profile

I am an experimental physicist working at the intersection of quantum materials, nanoscale characterization, and atomic-scale quantum sensing. My research spans superconducting and topological systems, electron dynamics probed by shot noise, artificial atomic structures, and spin-dependent phenomena in functional materials.

My career has connected complementary research environments: I currently work at Radboud University, previously held a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellowship at Université Paris-Saclay / CNRS, and completed my PhD research at the Indian Association for the Cultivation of Science. Across these stages, I have developed expertise in scanning probe experiments, low-temperature measurements, quantitative data analysis, scientific writing, and collaborative project development.

Research vision

From observing quantum states to engineering quantum functionality.

My long-term vision is to establish an experimental research programme that combines atomic-scale measurement with controlled construction of quantum matter. By resolving local electronic dynamics, spin states, and interactions at the level of individual atoms and molecules, I aim to identify the microscopic mechanisms that govern emergent quantum behaviour—and use that knowledge to design new sensing and information-processing concepts.

1 · Measure

Advance local probes, including tunnelling spectroscopy and shot-noise methods, to access fluctuations, lifetimes, correlations, and quantum states that are difficult to infer from average conductance alone.

2 · Engineer

Build artificial atomic and molecular structures with designed coupling, symmetry, and spin configurations, then test how controlled microscopic changes reshape their collective properties.

3 · Translate

Use the resulting physical insight to explore routes towards all-electrical quantum sensing, atomically defined neuromorphic elements, and spin-selective functional interfaces.

4 · Collaborate

Connect surface science, superconductivity, molecular materials, and quantum-device communities through shared experimental platforms and complementary theory.

Research independence & leadership

My experience combines fellowship-supported project leadership, development of experimental methods, interdisciplinary research, and mentoring of early-career researchers.

Independent fellowship project

As a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellow and project Principal Investigator at Université Paris-Saclay / CNRS, I conceived and led a research programme focused on atomic-scale sensing of Majorana zero modes and implications for fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Experimental method development

Led scanning tunnelling shot-noise experiments to investigate quantum degrees of freedom and electron dynamics at the atomic scale, including work on vibron-mediated electron bunching.

Cross-disciplinary research

My work bridges superconductivity and topological matter with nanoscale optoelectronics, perovskite materials, spin-dependent transport, and scanning probe microscopy.

Mentoring and communication

Guided PhD researchers in scanning probe microscopy, low-temperature measurements, data analysis, and manuscript preparation; presented research at international conferences and invited seminars.
