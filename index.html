About

Recent advances in condensed matter physics and material science are increasingly shaped by two major frontiers: nanotechnology and quantum materials. My research aims to bridge these fields by harnessing nanoscience to probe and engineer quantum materials with atomic precision. My favourite experimental technique is scanning tunnelling microscopy (STM), which enables atomic-scale imaging and probing electronic density of states. In addition, STM also offers the ability to manipulate indiviudal atoms on the surface and construct atomically precise nanostructures. Furthermore, by integrating high-frequency techniques into conventional STM setups, one can investigate time-domain phenomena and access dynamical information at the atomic scale. This makes it a powerful tool not only for exploring quantum phenomena at the atomic scale but also for engineering novel quantum systems with tailored functionalities. My long-term vision is to build quantum matter and technologies atom by atom, using individual quantum building blocks to create functionalities with long coherence time.

Research Interests

  • Atomic-Scale Quantum Sensing: Spin on Surfaces
    Using individual surface spins as local quantum sensors to probe atomic-scale magnetic phenomena, electronic states, and quantum correlations.
  • Artificial Quantum Materials: Hamiltonian Engineering
    Engineering atomic lattices and interatomic couplings to tailor electronic Hamiltonians and realise artificial quantum states with designed functionalities.
  • Atomic-Scale Quantum Transport
    Employing broadband scanning tunnelling spectroscopy (noise measurements, electronic pump-probe, spin resonance measurements) to probe charge-transport fluctuations, transport statistics, and dynamcial electronic correlations at the atomic scale.
