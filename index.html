Research Area: Atomic-Scale Quantum Nanoscience

In the last fifty years, the research community has made significant progress in realising Richard Feynman’s famous vision of ‘plenty of room at the bottom’ through the engineering of materials and their properties at the nanoscale. However, despite significant progress, we are still far from realising this vision in the context of another major breakthrough: the recent surge of interest in correlated quantum materials.

Solution-based processing, particularly wet-chemical synthesis, has been one of the major approaches to fabricating nanomaterials. However, correlated quantum materials present a fundamentally different challenge: their delicate quantum ground states are highly susceptible to decoherence and sensitive to atomic-scale disorder. Consequently, conventional bottom-up nanofabrication techniques may not provide the level of structural precision and control required to engineer and preserve these quantum states for practical applications, such as quantum computing.

The question that my current and future research is going to address: can we create, read and manipulate corelated quantum states with atomic precision and eventually build quantum matter and technologies atom-by-atom?

Experimental Approach

My favourite experimental technique is scanning tunnelling microscopy (STM), which enables atomic-scale imaging and probing electronic density of states. Another fascinating advantage of STM is its ability to manipulate individual surface atoms, enabling the fabrication of fully atomically precise nanostructures and artificial lattices.

Research Vision

I am particualrly interested to investigate atomic-scale objects (dopants, defects, adosrbates) on semiconducting and superconducting hosts. They can serve as a model quantum system to understand the coupling of fundamental degrees of freedom: charge, spin, lattice and orbit, which is thought to be responsible for the emergence of correlated quantum phases and exotic quasiparticles.

As a next step, I have recently begun exploring to manipulate these atomic objects and assemble them to form artifical matter (0D, 1D, 2D) where additional coupling between individual quantum states could enable the creation, enginerring and control of correlated quantum ground states. The long-term dream is therefore to achieve coherent qubit functionalities towards quantum sensing and computing applications.

Current Research Projects

  • Atomic-Scale Quantum Sensing: Spin on Surfaces
    The goal is to use a single spin on semiconductor surface as a local quantum sensor to detect neighbouring magnetic moments, map local magnetic interactions, and probe spin excitations and their coupling to the environment.
  • Artificial Quantum Materials: Flat-Band Engineering
    Creating artificial atomic lattices by charged adsorbates on semiconductor surface using STM-based atomic manipulation to engineer electronic structures with flat bands. Investigating how lattice geometry, symmetry, and interatomic coupling control flat-band formation, bandwidth, and wavefunction localization, and how the resulting enhanced density of states and electron–electron interactions influence the emergence of correlated electronic phases.
  • Atomic-Scale Quantum Transport
    Employing broadband scanning tunnelling spectroscopy, in particular current-noise measurements, to investigate quantum transport and dynamical correlations in defects/dopants on semicoductor and superconductor hosts with a particular focus on identifying signatures of exotic quasiparticles through their characteristic transport fluctuations, tunnelling spectra, and dynamical responses.
  • Atomic-Scale Neuromorphic Matter
    Engineering artificial atomic-scale systems with multistable energy landscapes to realise stochastic units of information (bits). Exploring how competing energy minima, electronic interactions, and thermally activated transitions can be harnessed to encode, store, and process information, paving the way for brain-inspired computing with atomic-scale quantum materials.
